Formulas Used

Angular velocity

The rate at which the rover wheel spins around an axis
w = s/rt
where:
w = angular velocity (rad/s)
s = displacement (m)
r = wheel radius (m)
t = time (s)

Propulsion force

The force generated by a motor to move the rover forward
F = n*T/r
where:
F = propulsion force (N)
T = torque force per wheel (N*m)
r = wheel radius (m)
n = number of wheels

maximum friction force

The theoretical fricition force that resists the rover's motion. This creates traction
Fs,max = μs*N
where:
Fs,max = Maximum friction force (N)
μs = static friction coefficient
N = normal force (N)
Normal force = mass * gravity(on mars: 3.71 m/s2) * cos(angle of inclination)
If the propulsion is greater than the maximum friciton, wheels of hte rover will slip. The motor is trying to push harder than the ground can transmit through static friction, so the wheel spins/slips.

Resistance force

The theoretical force resisting the rover's motion comprised of rolling resistance from the wheel's deformation and resistance from the slope.
Fresistance = Frolling + Fslope
where:
Fresistance = resistance force (N)
Frolling = rolling force (N) = Crr(rolling resistance coefficient) * N
Fslope = slope force (N) = mass * gravity (3.71 on mars) * sin(angle of inclinatoin)
N = normal force (N)
Normal force = mass * gravity(on mars: 3.71 m/s2) * cos(angle of inclination)

Net force

The theoretical total force acting on the rover
Fnet = Fpropulsion - Fresistance
where:
Fnet = net force (N)
Fpropulsion = propulsion force (N)
Fresistance = resistance force (N)
If the resistance force is less than the proplusion force, the rover will move. If net force is 0, the rover won't move. if it is less than 0 it will move in the opposite of the intended direction.

Work done

The force applied to the rover over a distance
W = Fnet * d
where:
W = work done (J)
Fnet = net force (N)
d = distance (m), this is a scalar version of displacement

Acceleration

The rate of change of velocity of the rover- in this model we will take it as a contant
a = Fnet / m
where:
a = acceleration (m/s2)
Fnet = net force (N)
m = mass (kg)

Time

The time it takes to cover the displacement
t = -u + √((u2+2as)/a)
where:
t = time (s)
u = initial velocity (m/s)
s = displacement (m)
a = acceleration (m/s2)

Final velocity

the velocity at the end of displacement after acceleration has been applied ot the initial velocity
v = u+a*t
where:
v = final velocity (m/s)
u = initial velocity (m/s)
a = acceleration (m/s2)
t = time (s)
